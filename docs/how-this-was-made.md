# How this project was made

This antenna switch is an experiment in designing hardware together with an AI assistant.
The assistant is Claude, made by Anthropic.

## Roles

**Prins Doornekamp** (owner)
- Set the goals, the requirements and the design rules.
- Took the decisions. The design log records the date of each decision and, in most cases, who decided.
- Brought the station knowledge, the bench equipment and the measurements.
- Reviewed the drawings and asked for changes.

**Claude** (AI assistant)
- Researched parts, datasheets and standards, and proposed options with their trade-offs.
- Did calculations (impedance, creepage, timing, derating).
- Wrote the Python scripts that generate the KiCad schematics, footprints and diagrams.
- Wrote the verification scripts (truth tables, netlist topology checks, timing).
- Kept the design log and drafted the documents.

The work was done in conversation. Many proposals were changed or rejected along the way.
The archive (rev A/B card cage and backplane) shows one such change of direction.
Credit for the design is shared.

## Method

1. Requirements and rules first (docs/requirements.md, design-log.md section 1).
2. For each choice: options, trade-offs, a recommendation, then a decision by Prins.
3. Every decision, assumption, calculation and measurement goes in the design log with its reason.
4. Schematics are generated by scripts, so each change is repeatable and can be compared.
5. Every logic circuit is checked with truth tables against the real netlist.
6. Bench tests confirm what the desk work cannot (docs/test/test-spec.md).

## Limits

- An AI can make mistakes that look correct. For this reason the design relies on scripted checks and on bench tests, not on trust.
- Some values in the design log are marked "assumed" or "to confirm". They are not yet verified.
- No board is built yet. The first bench results will show how well this method works.
