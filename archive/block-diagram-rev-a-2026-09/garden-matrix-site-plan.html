Garden Matrix Site Plan

The relay matrix lives in the stainless antenna box at the mast base, where every antenna arrives and the buried cables leave. The brains stay in the basement: a shack head with the controller and panel, talking to a link card in the garden over one Cat6. Everything that decides safety is duplicated on the garden side, so a cut cable, a hung head or a lost heartbeat leaves every antenna grounded. Card designs: matrix · radio port · antenna port · controller.

What moved, and why

ElementRev B position
Controller + panelBasement, in a small shack head. The ESP32, USB, Wi-Fi, OLED and buttons are the noisiest and most-touched parts of the system; none of them belong at the current maximum of a resonant grounded mast. The Controller card is unchanged except that its backplane connector becomes a link connector.
Link cardReplaces the controller in slot 0 of the garden backplane. Isolated RS-485 transceiver, a small bridge MCU that executes expander writes and reports back, and the full local safety chain: watchdog, supply supervisor, gate → /RLY_EN. Loss of link heartbeat for more than a second releases the gate.
PTTNever goes through firmware or the RS-485 protocol. The FT-710's TX GND drives a current loop on its own Cat6 pair; an opto on the link card puts it straight onto PTT[1] on the garden backplane, where K0 on Radio Port 1 and the RX-mute on Radio Port 2 follow it in hardware. Sequencing stays inside the FT-710's TX delay, as your RFI notes require. The Radio Port card's PTT source jumper is set to "backplane" instead of "front jack".
KILLA second Cat6 pair carries a current loop through the panel's KILL switch. Open loop, released gate, everything grounded. This is the physical kill switch the controller page left open, and it works with the head unpowered.
Power230 V is already at the box, so the garden side gets a linear supply: toroid, bridge, linear 12 V for relays and 5 V for logic. No switch-mode converter anywhere in the box. The transformer's few watts of idle dissipation also keep the box above dew point most of the year.
RX/TX switch and bias-TBoth designed-but-unbuilt items from the RFI log land here. K0 on Radio Port 1 is the sequenced TX/RX switch; the loop's preamp is fed through the bias-T option on Antenna Port 2, no extra conductor up the mast.
VHFGP-6N coax passes straight through on two bulkhead SO-239s with a choke. HF relays are not rated for 430 MHz and the FTM-510D has nothing to switch.

Limits this sets

  1. Three coaxes means three radio lines to the basement. A third HF transceiver needs a fourth coax or a shared line; the matrix has the slots, the ground does not have the cable.
  2. Every conductor entering the RVS box is a common-mode path at the noisiest point on the site. The rule is simple: nothing enters without a choke, nothing enters without isolation or surge protection, and nothing inside switches at kHz rates.
  3. Latency: RS-485 route changes take milliseconds, which is fine because routing never happens during a transmission. PTT and KILL are wires, not messages, so nothing safety-relevant depends on the protocol.

Open items

  1. Cat6 type in the ground: UTP or shielded. If shielded, decide where the shield bonds (box only, or both ends with the chokes doing the RF work). Ties into the mast-earth vs house-PE bonding question.
  2. Surge protection on the Cat6 at the box: gas-discharge on each pair to the box, plus TVS behind the isolation. The box sits at the base of the tallest metal object in the garden.
  3. Condensation strategy: rely on PSU self-heating plus a drain and breather, or add a small resistive heater on a thermostat. No fans.
  4. Relay class in a damp box: sealed (washable) relays rather than flux-tight, and conformal coating on the cards.
  5. Whether the link card's bridge MCU is the same ESP32-S3 with radio disabled (one firmware tree) or a tiny STM32G0/RP2040 (fewer things to go wrong outdoors).
