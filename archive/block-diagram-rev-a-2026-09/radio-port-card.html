Shack Relay Matrix · card architecture · rev A

Radio Port Card

One card per radio slot. The radio's single antenna socket comes in, a PTT-driven changeover relay splits it into a TX path and an RX path, and each path has its own row of six relays onto the RF bus lines. One PCB serves both the FT-710 and the Airspy: the RX-only build leaves the TX row empty and moves one jumper. Parent: Shack Relay Matrix · sibling: Antenna Port Card.

Blocks

BlockFunction and choice
K0 changeoverSPDT, COM to the radio, NO to the TX path, NC to the RX path. Idle is receive. Its coil is driven straight from the PTT opto, not through the MCU, and from the unswitched 12 V rail: the radio's own PTT decides which path it is on, always. Power-relay class, since it carries 100 W on every over. Needs the radio's TX delay to exceed its transfer time (FT-710: external-amp keying delay, set 20 to 30 ms).
TX row K1–K6Six SPST power relays, one per RF bus line. Firmware closes at most one. Unpopulated on an RX-only card.
RX row K7–K12Six SPST signal relays, one per line. Cheaper and better isolation than the power relays; they never carry transmit power on purpose. Jumper option: the row's driver enable is /PTT, so the RX antenna is disconnected during transmit. That protects the K0 NC contact from full TX voltage when TX and RX antenna are the same line, and stops the RX antenna re-radiating into the receiver path.
RF bus edgeSix edge connections to the RF bus board, one per antenna line. On the card, TX relay and RX relay for a line join at the edge pin. Keep the fan-out stubs short: five open relays hang on every line as capacitance.
TX sense detectorTap on the radio side of K0. RF present without PTT (VOX, CW keying not wired) or PTT without RF are both logged; RF while a matrix relay is mid-switch drops /RLY_EN.
PTT in + optoFront 3.5 mm jack, opto-isolated input that sinks the radio's open-collector or contact PTT (FT-710 TX GND, 80 mA rated). The opto output drives three things in hardware: this slot's PTT[n] line on the backplane (open-drain), the K0 driver, and an expander input for firmware lockout.
K0 drive selectJumper. TX radio: K0 follows local PTT. RX-only card: K0 follows the diode-OR of all four PTT[1..4] lines, so the receiver is lifted off its antenna whenever anything in the shack transmits. That is the TX-mute function, without a second relay.
I2C expanderPCA9555, 16 bit, address from SLOT_ID. Twelve outputs for K1–K12, LEDs; inputs PTT, RF_DET, DIP.
Relay drivers + rail switch12-channel low-side driver for the matrix relays. Their 12 V comes through a high-side MOSFET enabled by /RLY_EN. Firmware can set any pattern it likes; with the enable released no matrix coil has power. K0 is on the unswitched rail on purpose: it must keep following PTT even in a fault, otherwise a keyed radio could be left looking at an open RX path.
ID EEPROM + DIP24C02: radio name, TX-capable, max power, required TX delay. DIP: force RX-only, RX row opens on PTT. Hardware truth beats EEPROM; mismatch logged.
TX inhibit outOption. Open-collector to the radio's TX inhibit or TX REQ input where the radio has one, asserted whenever /RLY_EN is released or a matrix relay is moving. Belt to the TX-delay braces.

What the controller enforces here

  1. At most one relay closed per row. TX row and RX row may point at different lines (split antennas) or the same line (plain single antenna).
  2. No relay in either row moves while this slot's PTT is active, and not while RF_DET is high.
  3. Break before make with settle delay between rows and lines.
  4. The TX row of a TX-capable card is never routed to a line whose Antenna Port card is flagged RX-only.
  5. Coil budget: K0 plus at most two matrix relays energised per card, so a fully populated chassis stays under about 0.5 A on the relay rail.

Sequencing on key-down

Radio asserts PTT → opto → K0 to TX path and PTT[n] on the bus (≈5 ms relay transfer) → radio's TX delay expires → RF. If the RX-row-opens-on-PTT jumper is set, K7–K12 drop in the same window. Nothing in this chain waits for the MCU. The MCU's only job during a transmission is to refuse to move anything.

Open items

  1. K0 and TX-row relay part: 100 W at 50 Ω, hot-switch tolerance for the fault case, transfer time under 10 ms.
  2. Whether the RX-row-opens-on-PTT option is default on. Argues for: protects K0 contact and receiver. Against: relay wear on every over, and a small click on the RX path during break-in operation.
  3. FT-710 TX inhibit: confirm what the LINEAR jack actually offers (TX GND out, TX REQ in) before committing the option footprint.
  4. Detector threshold and whether the same detector should also give a coarse power reading via a comparator ladder or an ADC pin on the controller.
